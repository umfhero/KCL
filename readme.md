## Module details captured from the portal

### `7CCSMCIS` — Cryptography

- **Term:** First
- **Credits:** 15 at Level 7
- **Lecturer shown:** Dr Laurent Mouchard
- **Prerequisites:** None, although mathematical maturity is expected
- **Assessment:** 100% two-hour written examination; double marked; 50% module pass mark

The module combines the theory and implementation of cryptography, authentication, and information security. Its mathematical content requires an in-depth understanding for the examination.

Main topics include:

- modular arithmetic, multiplicative inverses, Euler's and Fermat's theorems, the Extended Euclidean Algorithm, and the Chinese Remainder Theorem;
- block and stream ciphers, modes of operation, DES, and AES;
- RSA, ElGamal, SHA, and MD5;
- Diffie–Hellman, Needham–Schroeder, Otway–Rees, and Kerberos;
- authentication, zero-knowledge identification, and Fiat–Shamir protocols;
- RSA, ElGamal, DSA, and DSS digital signatures; and
- passwords, malware, phishing, firewalls, buffer overflows, and denial-of-service attacks.

Suggested reading from the supplied description:

1. Paar and Pelzl, *Understanding Cryptography* (2010)
2. Ferguson, Schneier, and Kohno, *Cryptography Engineering* (2010)
3. Schneier, *Applied Cryptography* (1996)
4. [Handbook of Applied Cryptography](https://www.cacr.math.uwaterloo.ca/hac/)

### `7CCSMCFC` — Computer Forensics and Cybercrime

- **Term:** Second
- **Credits:** 15 at Level 7
- **Lecturer shown:** Dr Richard Overill
- **Prerequisites:** None
- **Assessment:** 100% 3,000-word dissertation; double marked; 50% module pass mark

The module covers computer crime and its social and legal implications, computer and network forensics, and their relationship to the UK Computer Misuse Act and related European legislation.

Main topics include:

- hacking, phishing, malware, and spyware;
- financial fraud, embezzlement, blackmail, and social engineering;
- information warfare and intrusion detection;
- computer, network, and meta-forensics; and
- the Computer Misuse Act and the Council of Europe Convention on Cybercrime.

Suggested reading from the supplied description includes works by Eoghan Casey, Dorothy Denning, Kruse and Heiser, Kevin Mitnick, Sammes and Jenkinson, Bruce Schneier, and Edward Wilding.

### `7CCSMPRJ` — MSc Individual Project

- **Period:** Principally February to September
- **Credits:** 60 at Level 7
- **Assessment:** 100% dissertation; double marked; 50% module pass mark
- **Formative work described:** preliminary report, brief oral presentation, and two progress reports

The project must be suitable for the programme and clearly at postgraduate level. It can be practical, theoretical, or a combination of both, but cannot consist mainly of a literature review. It must contain practical and/or theoretical results produced by the student.

A complete project should clearly explain:

- the technical background and relevant literature;
- its specific objectives and motivation;
- the student's technical contribution, including experimental and/or theoretical results; and
- conclusions, interpretation of the results, and possible extensions.

Where software or another artefact is produced, it should be submitted with instructions for use. The supplied description recommends TeX or LaTeX and says the dissertation should normally not exceed 40 single-spaced pages in 11-point text with reasonable margins.
